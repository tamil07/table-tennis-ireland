## Why

Leeside is the first club on the platform. The owner will run the platform personally and commit full time. The plan is to offer the platform to other table-tennis clubs in Ireland, and for clubs to pay for it. That needs repeatable onboarding, a clear operator role, a commercial model, and the legal and operational groundwork that comes with holding members' data, including juniors'. None of this is in the current change, which covers only the Leeside launch.

## What Changes

- Add a phased plan with explicit gates: Leeside proven first, then repeatable onboarding, then a paid pilot with one or two clubs, then general offering.
- Make onboarding of a new club a tool, not a manual database task: a parameterised club setup, an operator screen for creating clubs and approving their website addresses, and per-club branding.
- Define the operator role: the owner runs the platform personally, with documented access, recovery and backup responsibilities.
- Define the commercial model before outreach: what clubs pay, what support they receive, and the terms under which the platform processes their data.
- Put data protection, safeguarding and retention decisions in place before a second club joins.
- Keep styling separate from this plan. The choice between the current design-token approach and Tailwind is decided in its own visual-design change and does not gate any step here.

## Impact

- New operator tooling and per-club configuration, with cross-club isolation tests.
- Legal documents (terms, data processing agreement, privacy notice) and a retention schedule.
- Paid hosting and monitoring before the second club. Free tiers stay for Leeside only until the review in phase 2.
- No change to the Leeside launch scope. Its open owner actions remain in `launch-cork-club-platform` tasks section 14.
